# V1 dress rehearsal (2026-09-30) — mechanics only; nothing here counts toward V1

Two events **outside the pool**, closed before the model's cutoff (contaminated by design): the March 2026 Fed decision (exact partition, event 67284) and the February 2026 Bitcoin "hit" ladder (touch ladder, event 194107). Sessions: fresh, the declared operator model, spawned from the orchestrating conversation and sent only a one-line instruction to read a prompt file. Attempt 1 (prompts pasted by hand) is kept in `packets_rehearsal/attempt1_pasted_prompts/`; attempt 2 is the file-based mechanism that will be used.

## What worked
- **End to end:** prompt file, one read, answer handed back, answer ingested **from the session's own transcript** (never copied by hand), audited, menu built, follow-up turn in the same session, second answer ingested and audited, scored. All four session turns passed the audit (declared model; one read of the exact prompt file, chunked for the long one; content and instruction verified; no other tool).
- **Cost, measured** (the runs' own token counts): stage 1a about **44k to 47k** tokens per session; stage 1b a further **37k to 42k**; about **85k tokens per round**. For 24 rounds about **2.0M**; the reserve (up to 9 more rounds after replacements) up to **0.8M** more; a variance check on 6 rounds about **0.5M**. Wall-clock per session turn 20 to 30 seconds; the menu (2 minutes) and scoring price checks (several minutes) dominate and run in the background.

## What it found (each fixed or raised)
1. **Attempt 1's Bitcoin prompt was abbreviated by hand**; the audit's "prompt matches the file" check caught it. This is why prompts are now read from a file (I never carry them) and the audit compares the recorded instruction and the read content.
2. **The answer validator rejected any dollar figure or percent sign**, including strikes and rates taken from the rules. Narrowed to quantified or market-derived probability language only ("a 30% chance", "the probability of a cut is 0.3", "already priced in", "the market implies"). Numbers from the rules are allowed.
3. **A term-matched menu was 1 to 3 candidates** (the terms the session wrote matched almost nothing in a closed-and-resolved index). Stage 1b now shows **every live candidate** at the lock (65 and 71 in the rehearsal), after the session has written its hedge thesis; its terms are kept and the count they would have matched is reported (1 each).
4. **Tier mapping was limited to the 12 rungs shown**; it now maps over **every rung open at the lock**. **Duplicate rungs** (Polymarket relists contracts with identical text and different outcomes, and "(copy)" contracts) are ambiguous and are **not mapped to**; they are reported. A strike-parsing bug that read the "b" of "by" as a billion is fixed.
5. **The recall canary is noisy.** The same Fed session said it recalled the outcome in attempt 1 and did not in attempt 2; the Bitcoin session said so in attempt 1 and not in attempt 2 while inferring the early-February crash from the contract windows. It catches some recall, not all. The protection that does not depend on the model's honesty is the pool rule (resolved after the cutoff).
6. **A contract list leaks the underlying's level at the lock.** The Bitcoin session inferred where Bitcoin had traded from which strikes existed and when they were relisted. That is information available at the lock (not the outcome), and it is consistent with "price is attentional, not out of the system", but it means a session is never wholly price-blind on a ladder.
7. **A claim did not match its hedge.** In the Fed round the session held a gold hedge at the moderate tier and made its claim about the mild tier, so the claim credited no hedge and the floor was not lifted (a claim about something not held does nothing). Raised for Rob below.
8. **The harness wraps follow-up messages** and injects reminders as user turns; the audit strips both.
