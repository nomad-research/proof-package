This briefing is self-contained, so it can be pasted or uploaded into a chat window to discuss the design. It is current to Rob's message of 2026-10-01 asking for it. The full record is in the repository (`STATE.md`, `docs/nomad_v18_addendum_B1.md`, `docs/COMPONENTS_REGISTER.md`, `lookbacks/polymarket/`).

## 1. What Nomad is now

Nomad reads public documents about geopolitical and adjacent events and turns the reading into positions on Polymarket event contracts. Nothing places an order: every result so far is on paper, against recorded prices and real resolutions.

| Rule | What it says |
|---|---|
| R6 | Price is never evidence for a thesis or a probability. It enters only at expression: which contract, what it costs, how much to hold, and scoring. Sessions write their reading without seeing prices. |
| R20 | Results are read for direction first: the effect, its 90% interval, and the probability that it is positive. |

## 2. What the evidence says

### 2.1 Hedging (studies V1 and V1b)

The test asked whether the sessions' own hedges beat simple de-risking. 41 rounds were scored, and the pooled figures are post-hoc.

| Result | Figure |
|---|---|
| V1 | 17 scored: inconclusive |
| V1b | 24 scored: inconclusive |
| Session hedge minus de-risking, pooled | +2.7 (−2.0 to +7.5) |
| Same-underlying hedges | +9.1 (+0.4 to +21.8) |
| Cross-entity hedges | +0.9 (nothing) |
| Hedge timing (hedges pay more when the main position loses) | +9.1, p 0.063 |
| Session hedge against a blind statistical hedge | Session hedge better in 12 of 15 rounds, +14.9 (+4.1 to +24.3) |

**Verdict** (`lookbacks/polymarket/EVALUATION.md`): hedging as a source of edge over de-risking is not supported. Arithmetically it cannot be one: with single-event contracts, expected profit is the sum of each position's belief minus its price, and hedging changes only risk.

### 2.2 The sessions' picks (T0)

These are the sessions' own "what will happen" calls, bet at the lock.

| Result | Figure |
|---|---|
| All picks: hit minus all-in cost | −0.3 points |
| Picks against matched-price alternatives | −2.3 points, P(positive) 0.19 |
| Favourites priced above 0.8 | +2 points, P 0.89 |

The picks show no edge over price. The favourite result looks like the market's known habit of underpricing favourites and is held only as a hypothesis.

### 2.3 Claims ("if A then B", T0)

This is the strongest result Nomad has.

- **When A happened.** "Then" held in 71% of claims against a 48% lock price (14 claims, de-duplicated): +24 points (+5 to +42), P 0.98. The claims beat a matched placebo by +22 points, P 0.96, so the sessions picked the right linked contract.
- **When A did not happen.** B held 33% against a 38% price.
- **The edge is entirely in magnitude claims.** This is a post-hoc split.

  | Claims stated as | Held when A happened | Mean lock price |
  |---|---|---|
  | Direction plus severity tier (mild, moderate, severe) | 8 of 9 | 0.43 |
  | A named contract | 2 of 5 | 0.55 |

  The only magnitude miss was a timing error. Both wrong-direction failures were named-contract claims.
- **Waiting for A and then buying B does not work.** Half the time B resolves first, and the rest of the time B's price moves within about an hour.

### 2.4 Every claim's "then" bet at the lock, under three weightings (post-hoc)

Each "then" contract was bought at the lock, before anything resolved. Figures are return on money staked, resampled by round.

| | Equal shares | Equal variance | Equal stake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magnitude claims (47) | +27%, P 0.83 | +37%, P 0.88 | +69%, P 0.88 |
| Named-contract claims (22) | −6% | −2% | −36% |
| Picks (143) | 0% | +1% | −4% |

The magnitude claims are positive under every weighting. Equal stake rests on three long shots priced near 0.08, and every interval spans zero.

## 3. The design as of today

**Graph and baskets**
- **A thesis is a subgraph.** Events are nodes, and dependencies between them are edges.
- **Each subgraph is one basket.** The basket is *which* positions (contracts, and the side taken on each), not how much of each.
- **Links are never traded on their own.** They shape the subgraph.
- **Claims are edges.** Each carries both branches ("if A" and "if not A") and is scored for calibration.

**Sizing**
- **Sizing is suspended.** It was never discussed, so v18's Kelly sizer and its values are proposals only and are not built. Tests score baskets unsized.
- **Position risk and global risk are separate.** A basket's stake = position weight × global scale.
  - **Position risk:** what one basket can lose and how its payoff is shaped. It needs only prices and the subgraph.
  - **Global risk:** how much of the bankroll is at risk at once. It covers the total deployed, groups of baskets that share a driver, money locked until resolution, and drawdown.
  - **Global risk comes down to four numbers,** which are Rob's and blank:
    1. the most deployed at once;
    2. the most on one basket;
    3. the most on a group sharing a driver;
    4. the drawdown stop.
- **Magnitude should drive sizing** (Rob). It is still open whether magnitude is something the session authors (how far, how many, how soon) or something derived from risk (the multiple a basket returns on its risk).

**Unratified values are missing components,** not thresholds to assume.
- **The register sorts about 107 values.** It lists 8 components to discuss, about 9 test design values, the values frozen in finished studies, and the 83 values of the parked equity harness, which do not apply.
- **Nothing new goes in** without being logged as a gap in a named component.

## 5. Suggested discussion: four hand picks, in order

**1. What the session writes: magnitude and belief (C3 and C5).** This comes first because T3 must be authored by 14 October, and its packet decides what data we collect.
- Should sessions state magnitude in world units ("crude above $95 by December", "at least 12 seats"), rather than three tier words that the builder maps to rungs by price?
- Should they also give a probability, so their calibration can be measured, or is magnitude enough?
- What should a session write for a yes/no event with no scale?

**2. How magnitude becomes size (C3 and C2).**
- Is magnitude authored, or derived from risk?
- Does a bigger magnitude mean a bigger position, or a position further out at the same stake?
- Which weighting fits what Rob wants: equal shares, equal variance or equal stake? The last is lumpy: most baskets lose and a few pay many times over.

**3. Global risk (C1).** Only needed before money, but it frames the rest.
- The four numbers.
- What counts as a shared driver.
- How to set a drawdown stop that does not trigger on the normal losing streaks of far-rung bets.

**4. Basket boundaries (C4).**
- Can two baskets hold the same contract? A hedge as its own parallel thesis says yes; independent subgraphs say no.
- Who draws the subgraph: the session, the world graph, or both?

After these, the build can resume:
1. the shared packet, projection and scorer;
2. BT-A, the audit of whether backtest months are clean of the model having already seen the outcome;
3. T3;
4. V3 scoring from 15 October.

## 6. Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Event, contract, rung | An event is a question with several contracts; a ladder's contracts are rungs (for example "crude above $90", "above $95") |
| Lock | The moment a position would be taken; everything before it is knowable, nothing after |
| All-in cost | Price plus slippage, rounded up to the tick, plus the market's fee |
| Thesis, subgraph | A connected set of events with a view on how they resolve together |
| Basket | The set of positions a subgraph implies (which contracts, which side) |
| Claim | An edge "if A then B" inside a subgraph |
| Tier | A severity word (mild, moderate, severe) for how far a ladder moves |
| S and X hedge | Same underlying as the main position, or a different entity |
| Position risk, global risk | One basket's loss and payoff shape; the book's total exposure, shared drivers and drawdown |
| Direction read (R20) | Effect, 90% interval, and P(positive); 0.80 currently marks "push further" |
| Pre-registration | The test's rules and statistics, committed before any data is seen |
